How to answer

  • Write the whole phrase: verb + preposition + noun, exactly as Italian has it. Include the article when Italian uses one (al cinema, con un amico) and leave it out when it doesn't (a scuola, a casa).
  • Infinitive phrases ("To go…") need the infinitive: andare al cinema.
  • Present tense phrases ("She goes…") need the verb conjugated for that subject: va al cinema.
  • The subject pronoun is not required (io, tu, lui/lei, noi, voi, loro), but it is accepted if you add it.
  • Subjects: I = io, you = tu, he / she = third person singular (lui / lei, same verb form), we = noi, y'all = voi, they = loro.
  • Stuck on a noun? Click the underlined English noun in the phrase to see it in Italian (without the article).
  • After each answer you can mark the phrase Learned (never shown again) or postpone it to be repeated in 1, 3 or 5 days. Hotkeys after an answer: E learned, 1 / 2 / 3 repeat in 1 / 3 / 5 days, Enter next phrase. Your progress is saved in this browser.
  • Accents count (dà ≠ da); use the buttons under the field. Capital letters and extra spaces don't matter.

Articulated prepositions (preposition + article)

When a, di, da, in or su meet a definite article, they merge into one word.

  • il (plural i): masculine nouns starting with most consonants (il cinema, i libri).
  • lo (plural gli): masculine nouns starting with s + consonant, z, gn or ps (lo studio, gli studenti). gli is also the plural before vowels (gli amici).
  • l': singular nouns starting with a vowel, masculine or feminine (l'ufficio, l'università).
  • la, le: feminine nouns (la scuola, le vacanze).
  • con, per, tra/fra normally stay separate: con il professore, per la cena.
  • Examples: al cinema, dal medico, nel parco, sul treno, dell'ufficio.
